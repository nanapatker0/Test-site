MODULE 1: ACTING & PRESENTATION

1.1 Acting for Camera

Concept

Acting for the camera requires an understanding of scale, physical restraint, and spatial geometry. Unlike theater, where performances must project to the back of an auditorium, camera acting relies on proximity, micro-expressions, and spatial awareness relative to a dynamic lens.

Detailed Theory

  • What is Acting? Living truthfully under imaginary circumstances. Requires internalizing character motivation (super-objective), scene objectives, and tactics.
  • Stage vs. Camera Acting: Stage relies on broad gestures and vocal projection; camera acting is internal, restrained, and relies on subtle facial details and vocal intimacy.
  • Natural Performance: Achieved through active listening rather than waiting for dialogue cues.
  • Facial Expressions & Gestures: Magnified by the camera lens; gestures must stay within framing boundaries.
  • Eyeline: Spatial vector aligning actor's gaze with off-camera subjects across edits.

MODULE 6: ELEMENTS OF EDITING

Cut Type Edit Characteristics & Function
Straight Cut Standard hard cut; continuous transition between two shots.
Jump Cut Cut between two identical setups with minor time jump.
Match Cut Cut linking two shots via matching shapes, actions, or themes.
Cross Cut Alternates between two actions happening simultaneously.
Smash Cut Abrupt transition from quiet to intense (or vice versa).

SEMESTER 6 — MASTER GLOSSARY & REVISION

  • 180-Degree Rule: Preserves spatial consistency by keeping camera setups on one side of the axis line.
  • Aperture: Lens diaphragm opening controlling light intake and depth of field ($f$-stops).
  • Chiaroscuro: High contrast lighting emphasizing bold shadows and strong highlights.
  • Dynamic Range: Span between brightest highlights and darkest shadows captured without clipping (measured in stops).
  • Inverse-Square Law: Light intensity drops exponentially with distance ($I = P / 4\pi d^2$).
  • Mise-en-Scène: Everything placed in front of the camera (lighting, props, costumes, staging).
